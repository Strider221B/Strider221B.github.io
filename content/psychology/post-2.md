# Heuristics and Biases in Algorithmic Decision-Making

Human cognition relies heavily on dual-process systems: rapid, heuristic-driven intuition (System 1) and deliberative, logical analysis (System 2) as formalized by Kahneman and Tversky.

---

## 1. Automation Bias

When humans collaborate with intelligent systems, they tend to over-rely on automated recommendations:
- **Omission errors**: Failing to notice an automated system error because verification requires active effort.
- **Commission errors**: Implementing an incorrect automated recommendation even when contradictory data is present.

---

## 2. Anchoring on Initial Metrics

Data scientists and product managers are not immune to cognitive biases. An initial benchmark metric or baseline accuracy number often acts as an unconscious anchor, preventing radical architectural departures that might unlock superior solutions.

---

## Designing Bias-Aware Workflows

1. Present confidence intervals alongside point predictions.
2. Introduce lightweight friction or "cooling off" checks before executing irreversible automated decisions.
3. Explicitly visualize counterfactuals to combat confirmation bias.
