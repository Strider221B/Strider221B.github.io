### Don't Just Bring Problems, Bring Solutions: A Simple Trick for Career Success

One of the simplest yet most effective strategies for career advancement comes down to how you handle workplace challenges with your manager. 

When faced with a difficult issue, the natural instinct for many is to immediately escalate it to a supervisor. However, doing this consistently can backfire. Over time, your supervisor may begin to see you as a source of problems rather than a source of value, leading them to inadvertently avoid you when workloads peak. 

The proper approach? **Never take a problem to your manager without bringing a potential solution along with it.** 

### The Wrong Approach: Passing the Ball

Imagine a scenario where a team member is delivering work that has significant quality concerns. 

* **What many people do:** Go straight to the manager to complain about the individual's performance.
* **Why it fails:** By doing this, you are dumping the problem into your manager's lap. Everyone in modern workplaces is busy, and as people move higher up the corporate ladder, their time becomes increasingly scarce. Now, your manager has to pause their own critical tasks to investigate and figure out a resolution from scratch.

### The Right Approach: Strategic Brainstorming

Instead of passing the ball, take a moment to analyze the situation and identify the root cause before scheduling that meeting. 

Using the same example, suppose you dig a bit deeper and discover that the employee actually has exceptional design and coding skills, but they are brand new to your industry's specific domain. Their mistakes aren't driven by a lack of talent, but rather a lack of context. 

When you approach your supervisor, your conversation shifts from a complaint to a strategic strategy session: 

1. **State the Problem Clearly:** Outline the quality gap objectively.
2. **Offer the Root Cause:** Explain that the gaps stem from a lack of domain familiarity, not poor technical skills.
3. **Propose the Solution:** Suggest a targeted fix, such as organizing structured domain onboarding sessions for the employee.

### Why Managers Love Solution-Oriented Employees

Your supervisor might not always agree with your exact proposal, and that is completely fine. What matters is that you did the heavy lifting upfront. 

By brainstorming potential fixes beforehand, you demonstrate leadership, respect their limited time, and position yourself as a proactive problem-solver. It transforms a potentially negative meeting into a collaborative, time-saving brainstorming session—the exact kind of initiative that gets noticed when it time for promotions.
